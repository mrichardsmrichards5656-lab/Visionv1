What is Diabetic Retinopathy?

Diabetic retinopathy is the most common cause of preventable blindness in working-age adults in the UK, affecting over 1.7 million people. It is caused by high blood sugar damaging the retinal blood vessels. Anyone with Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes is at risk.

How it affects vision

In early stages there are often no symptoms — making annual screening critical. As it progresses: floaters, blurred vision, fluctuating vision, and washed-out colours. Sudden vision loss is a medical emergency.

Getting a diagnosis

Annual NHS diabetic eye screening (free for all people with diabetes) uses fundus photography to detect changes before symptoms appear.

Who it affects

Anyone with Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes. Risk increases with longer duration, poor blood sugar control, high blood pressure, and high cholesterol.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Faricimab (Vabysmo) — Fewer Injections

Dual-pathway injection approved by NICE for diabetic macular oedema. Targets both VEGF and Ang-2. Up to every 4 months for eligible patients — significantly fewer clinic visits than older treatments.

✅ NHS Available

Annual Eye Screening — Catch It Early

NHS diabetic eye screening detects retinopathy before you notice any symptoms, when treatment works best. Free for everyone with diabetes aged 12 and over — try not to miss your appointment.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

Annexin A5 (ANXV) — NEXUS Trial (London)

A novel drug using a human protein (Annexin A5) to protect retinal cells from damage and reduce inflammation — a completely different approach to anti-VEGF injections. The Phase 2a NEXUS trial is recruiting at The Retina Clinic London (started November 2025). Early findings from the first patients (reported February-April 2026) showed no safety concerns; primary completion is expected around mid-2026.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

Gene Therapy for Diabetic Retinopathy

Single-injection approaches delivering continuous anti-VEGF production are being developed — potentially replacing years of repeat injections. Leading programmes include ABBV-RGX-314 (sura-vec, in Phase 2/3 trials), 4D-150 (whose SPECTRA trial in diabetic macular oedema showed strong injection reduction) and ixo-vec. Most are in Phase 2-3 for diabetic eye disease, with regulatory submissions anticipated over the next few years.

Practical guides

Never Miss Your Annual Eye Screening

Annual NHS diabetic eye screening is free and takes 30 minutes. It detects retinopathy before you notice symptoms, when treatment is most effective.

Blood Sugar Control Protects Your Eyes

Every 1% reduction in HbA1c reduces the risk of microvascular complications (including retinopathy) by approximately 37% (UKPDS 35). CGMs (Libre 3, Dexcom G7) are often NHS-funded for Type 1.

Recognising an Emergency

Sudden vision loss, a curtain across your vision, or a sudden shower of floaters/flashing lights requires immediate A&E attendance.

Helpful technology

Blood Sugar Control

Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM)

A small arm-worn sensor tracking glucose continuously — the blood-sugar control it supports is the single biggest factor in slowing retinopathy. Current NHS devices include FreeStyle Libre 2 Plus / Libre 3 Plus and Dexcom G7 (Dexcom G6 is being phased out from 2026). NHS-funded if you meet NICE eligibility (NG17 type 1, NG28 type 2 on insulin).

Magnification

Magnifiers (Optical, Handheld & Desktop)

As retinopathy affects vision, optical magnifiers may be loaned free via an NHS low-vision clinic; electronic handheld magnifiers add adjustable contrast and colour, and desktop CCTV magnifiers suit longer reading.

AI Wearable

OrCam / Ray-Ban Meta + Be My Eyes

OrCam MyEye Smart clips to glasses and reads text and recognises faces/products; mainstream Ray-Ban Meta glasses with Be My Eyes describe surroundings and connect to a sighted helper at far lower cost.

Free App

Seeing AI / Be My Eyes

Seeing AI reads text and identifies people, products and currency; Be My Eyes connects to a sighted volunteer for live help. Both free, on iOS and Android.

Screen Settings

Larger Text, Contrast & Zoom

Built-in accessibility settings on phones, tablets and computers enlarge text, boost contrast and zoom the screen at no cost — useful for managing diabetes apps and CGM readings too.

Benefits & registration

Free Annual NHS Eye Screening

The most important thing you can do. Free for all people with diabetes. Never miss it.

PIP for Diabetic Complications

Vision loss from retinopathy supports a PIP claim. Diabetes UK has a detailed benefits guide at diabetes.org.uk.

Living with Diabetic Retinopathy

Never Miss Your Annual Screening

Annual NHS diabetic eye screening is the single most important thing you can do. It takes 30 minutes and detects changes before you notice symptoms. Missing even one year can allow significant damage to develop silently.

Blood Sugar Monitoring

A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) — Libre 3 or Dexcom G7 — makes maintaining good blood sugar control significantly easier and reduces diabetic retinopathy risk. Ask your diabetes team whether you are eligible for NHS funding.

Recognising an Emergency

Sudden vision loss, a curtain across your vision, or a sudden shower of floaters requires immediate A&E attendance — not a routine appointment. Never wait and see with sudden vision changes if you have diabetes.

Talking to Your Diabetes Team

Your diabetes nurse or consultant should know about any eye changes. Good coordination between your ophthalmologist and diabetes team is important — ask for a copy of your eye screening results to share at your diabetes appointments.

Emotional Impact

Managing diabetes is relentless, and the fear of sight loss adds real psychological burden. Diabetes UK has a dedicated emotional support service. RNIB (0303 123 9999) can help if vision has already been affected.
