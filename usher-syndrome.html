What is Usher Syndrome?

Usher syndrome is the most common condition involving both hearing loss and vision loss, affecting approximately 4–17 per 100,000 people (Retina UK). It combines sensorineural hearing loss with retinitis pigmentosa. There are three types. Usher syndrome accounts for around 50% of all hereditary deaf-blindness cases. Sense estimates around 10,000 people in the UK have Usher syndrome.

How it affects vision

The vision component follows a typical RP pattern: night blindness first, then progressive narrowing of peripheral vision, then central vision loss. The combination of vision and hearing loss creates a profound challenge to communication, navigation, and independence.

Getting a diagnosis

Diagnosed by audiological assessment, electroretinography (ERG), visual field testing, and genetic testing. Genetic testing identifies the specific gene and is essential for trial eligibility.

Who it affects

Affects approximately 4–17 per 100,000 people across populations (Retina UK). Type 2 is the most common form. Usually identified in childhood or teenage years. Affects both sexes equally.

Research & treatment progress

🔬 Pipeline

Ultevursen (QR-421a) — USH2A RNA Therapy

An RNA antisense therapy targeting exon 13 mutations in the USH2A gene (the most common cause of Usher type 2). It skips the faulty exon to produce a shorter but functional usherin protein, given as an eye injection. Originally developed by ProQR, it is now being advanced by Sepul Bio (part of Laboratoires Théa) in the Phase 2b LUNA trial, which has dosed its first patients.

🔬 Pipeline

MYO7A Gene Therapy — Type 1 Usher

MYO7A is the most common gene affected in Usher syndrome type 1. Because the gene is large, a dual-AAV approach is used to deliver it. AAVantgarde Bio's AAVB-081 (LUCE-1 trial) dosed its first patient in late 2024, with early safety reported as acceptable and an FDA Orphan Drug Designation granted. Still at Phase 1/2 stage.

🌅 Horizon

Combined Hearing and Vision Treatment

Long-term research into combined approaches addressing both the hearing loss and vision loss components of Usher syndrome simultaneously. Cochlear implants already help many with hearing — vision therapy is now catching up.

Practical guides

Deafblind Manual Communication

As vision and hearing both decline, deafblind manual communication becomes essential. Social services must fund communication support — request a specialist deafblind assessment from your local authority.

Connecting with an Intervenor

An intervenor is a specialist support worker trained in deafblind communication. Contact Sense (the deafblind charity) for guidance on accessing this support.

Getting a Genetic Test

Your Usher gene type determines which trials you can access. Free on the NHS — ask for referral to an inherited retinal disease clinic.

Helpful technology

Vibrating Alert

Vibrating Alert & Alarm Systems

Vibrating pagers and pads alert you to the doorbell, phone, smoke alarm, or a baby monitor through touch rather than sound or sight. Widely available from deafblind and deaf-equipment suppliers.

Communication

Refreshable Braille Display

Paired with VoiceOver or TalkBack, a braille display gives silent, tactile access to phone and computer content — invaluable when both hearing and sight are affected. Budget models like the Orbit Reader 20 start lower; 40-cell models cost more.

Screen Reader

Screen Readers (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA)

Built-in screen readers on iPhone/iPad (VoiceOver) and Android (TalkBack) are free, as is NVDA for Windows. Combined with a braille display they allow tactile reading without relying on hearing.

Hearing

Hearing Aids & Cochlear Implants

Most people with Usher have hearing loss from birth or early on. Hearing aids (free on the NHS) help in types 2 and 3; cochlear implants are offered on the NHS for severe-to-profound loss, including many children with type 1. Discuss with your audiology team.

Communication

Deafblind Manual & Communicator-Guides

Communication methods such as the deafblind manual alphabet, block alphabet, and hands-on signing, plus trained communicator-guides for getting out and about. Deafblind UK and Sense can arrange training and support.

Free App

Be My Eyes / Seeing AI

Be My Eyes connects you by live video to a sighted helper; Seeing AI reads text and describes surroundings through the camera. Useful for visual tasks as central vision narrows.

Benefits & registration

PIP Enhanced Rate (Both Components)

Usher syndrome almost always qualifies for the enhanced rate for both Daily Living and Mobility.

Local Authority Deafblind Support

Under the Care Act 2014, local authorities must meet eligible care needs. Request a deafblind assessment — this can fund intervenors and specialist equipment.

Disabled Students Allowance

Covers interpreters, notetakers, screen readers, and specialist software for students in higher education.

Living with Usher Syndrome

Communication as Vision Declines

Plan communication strategies before you need them — not during a crisis. Deafblind manual (tactile signing), hands-on signing, and braille are all options depending on your preferred communication. Sense (sense.org.uk) provides specialist communication support and can connect you with an intervenor.

Lighting and Contrast

Good lighting dramatically helps people with Usher syndrome — particularly in the evening and in unfamiliar environments. Install motion-sensor lights throughout the home. High contrast tape on step edges and door frames improves navigation significantly.

Technology for Dual Sensory Loss

A refreshable braille display paired with VoiceOver or TalkBack provides silent, tactile access to all phone content — messages, calls, apps — when both sight and hearing are significantly affected. Vibrating alert systems replace auditory signals for doorbells, alarms, and phones.

Emotional and Social Impact

The combination of hearing and vision loss creates a profound sense of isolation that others often find hard to understand. Sense runs social groups and residential events specifically for deafblind people — connecting with others who share your experience is invaluable.

Planning Ahead

Usher syndrome is progressive — planning for future needs while function is good is much easier than planning in a crisis. Review your communication strategies, home adaptations, and support arrangements at each major change in vision or hearing.

For Parents — Usher Syndrome

Usher syndrome combines hearing loss with progressive vision loss. If your child has been diagnosed, this section addresses the specific challenges and opportunities of this combination.

The Hearing Loss Comes First

Most children with Usher syndrome are identified through their hearing loss — often at birth screening. The vision loss (retinitis pigmentosa pattern) typically begins in the teenage years. This gives you time to prepare, build communication strategies, and ensure your child is connected to the right support.

Communication Planning is Essential

As vision declines alongside hearing, communication needs will change. Establish connections with Sense (the deafblind charity) early — they can help you plan communication strategies before they are urgently needed, rather than during a crisis.

Genetic Testing

Usher syndrome has multiple genetic types, each with different trial eligibility. USH2A trials (the most common form) are progressing rapidly. Genetic testing is free on the NHS — essential for trial access.

Connecting with Sense

Sense (sense.org.uk) is the UK's leading deafblind charity and has specialist services specifically for children and young people with Usher syndrome and their families. Their support can be life-changing.

Your Child's Social World

Usher syndrome can lead to social isolation as vision and hearing both decline. Building a strong social network, ensuring friends and school understand the condition, and connecting with other young people with Usher syndrome are all important. Retina UK and Sense both have youth communities.
