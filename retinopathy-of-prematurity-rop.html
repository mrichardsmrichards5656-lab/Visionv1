What is Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP)?

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) affects the blood vessels of the retina, the light-detecting layer at the back of the eye, in babies who are born early. Most ROP is mild and settles on its own without affecting sight. Around one in twenty very premature babies may develop severe ROP, which can cause partial or total loss of sight if it is not treated.

How it affects vision

ROP has no signs that parents can see, which is why babies at risk are screened. Mild ROP is unlikely to affect vision. Severe ROP can permanently damage the retina. Children who had ROP are more likely to be short-sighted or to develop a squint as they grow, so glasses are quite likely later. Premature babies can also have cerebral visual impairment, caused by injury to the brain, even without ROP.

Getting a diagnosis

Babies born before 32 weeks of pregnancy, or weighing under 1,500g at birth, are offered screening on the NHS. An ophthalmologist examines the back of the eyes at the cotside after drops to widen the pupils, using a headlight and lens or a special camera, with numbing drops and comfort measures such as swaddling. Most babies need more than one examination, and if ROP is found the eyes are checked again one to two weeks later. Screening may continue as an outpatient after your baby goes home.

Who it affects

ROP mainly affects very premature and very low birthweight babies. It is found in about 60 in 100 babies weighing under 1,501g, and in most of them it is mild.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Screening on the Neonatal Unit

Babies born before 32 weeks or weighing under 1,500g are examined regularly by an ophthalmologist until the risk has passed. Screening and treatment can usually prevent the most serious stages, where the retina detaches. Staff should arrange any outpatient eye appointments before your baby is discharged.

✅ NHS Available

Laser Treatment

Laser is the usual first treatment for severe ROP and reverses it about 90% of the time. Treatment should usually happen within 48 hours of diagnosis, and both eyes are usually treated together under sedation or a general anaesthetic. About 1 in 10 babies need a second treatment. If severe ROP is treated early, up to 80% of babies have good or very good eyesight.

✅ NHS Available

Anti-VEGF Injection (Ranibizumab)

Since July 2023 the NHS in England has routinely offered ranibizumab, an injection into the eye, for babies with severe ROP who are too unwell or fragile for laser, and as an alternative for some forms of ROP. Up to one in three babies need a second treatment within four months, so follow-up visits continue for several months. Ask your unit what is offered in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Practical guides

Keep Every Eye Appointment

ROP cannot be seen from the outside and the timing of checks matters. If your baby goes home before screening is finished, an outpatient appointment should be arranged before discharge. Please attend every one, and ring the unit if an appointment has not arrived.

What the Examination Is Like

Drops are given about an hour beforehand to widen the pupils. A small clip holds the eyelids open and numbing drops are used. Babies often cry, and the eyes may look red and puffy for a few hours afterwards. Staff use comfort measures such as swaddling and small amounts of milk or sugar drops. Ask the unit how you can help comfort your baby.

If Treatment Is Needed

The ophthalmologist will explain the options and ask for your written consent. Your baby may need to be moved to another hospital for treatment. Afterwards the eyes may look red and swollen and your baby is given eye drops. The eyes are checked again between two days and a week later.

Watch for Squint and Short Sight Later

Children who had ROP are more likely to need glasses or to develop a squint. Tell your health visitor, GP or optician if an eye seems to turn, or if your child does not seem to see well. NHS sight tests are free for children under 16.

Ask About Support at Nursery and School

Ask your local authority's vision impairment (sensory) service about an assessment. A Qualified Teacher of Children and Young People with Vision Impairment (QTVI) may be able to advise the nursery or school. Support is assessed and varies between areas, so ask early. Contact's free helpline for families of disabled children is 0808 808 3555.

Helpful technology

Magnifiers (Optical, Handheld & Desktop)

Two free smartphone apps. Seeing AI uses the phone camera to read text aloud and describe products and scenes; Be My Eyes connects you with a sighted volunteer by video for help with a task.

Benefits & registration

Disability Living Allowance for Children

A child under 16 who needs more care or supervision than other children of the same age because of their sight may qualify for Disability Living Allowance. It is assessed on your child's needs, not the diagnosis, and a decision can be challenged. Apply at gov.uk/disability-living-allowance-children. In Scotland, Child Disability Payment replaces it: apply through Social Security Scotland on 0800 182 2222.

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

Ask your consultant ophthalmologist about a Certificate of Vision Impairment, which is free. Registration can open access to local authority rehabilitation and support, free NHS sight tests and travel concessions. A council tax reduction is sometimes possible but is not automatic, so ask your council. Whether you qualify depends on how much sight you have, and your consultant decides.

Free NHS Sight Tests

Sight tests are free on the NHS if you are aged 60 or over (throughout the UK), and for everyone in Scotland. They are also free for people with diabetes or glaucoma, people registered as sight impaired, and some others, including children under 16. Ask your optician.

Living with Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP)

Glasses and Squint

Short sight and squint are more common after ROP and are usually picked up at follow-up or by an optician. Children under 16 may qualify for NHS optical vouchers towards glasses.

Nursery and School

Tell the nursery or school about your child's sight early. Large print, good lighting, sitting near the front and extra time are common adjustments. Extra support is assessed by the school and local authority rather than automatic, and you can ask for an assessment.

For Parents — Retinopathy of Prematurity

Hearing that your premature baby needs eye checks is one more worry at a hard time. Most ROP is mild and never needs treatment, and screening exists so that the few babies who need treatment get it in time.

Most ROP Is Mild

ROP is found in about 60 in 100 of the smallest babies, and in most it settles on its own. Around one in twenty very premature babies develop severe ROP.

Ask Who Your Contact Is

Ask the neonatal unit for the name and number of the person to ring about eye screening, and keep it with your baby's discharge papers.

Treatment Works Best Early

When treatment is needed it is usually done within 48 hours. Laser reverses severe ROP about 90% of the time.
