What is Age-Related Macular Degeneration?

AMD is the leading cause of vision loss in people over 50 in the UK, affecting around 700,000 people (Macular Society). It damages the macula — the central part of the retina. Dry AMD progresses slowly; Wet AMD is less common but more rapid and requires urgent treatment — same-day or next-working-day contact with your eye clinic if you notice sudden changes.

How it affects vision

Central vision becomes blurred or distorted. Straight lines may appear wavy — test with an Amsler grid daily. A dark or empty patch may develop in the centre of vision. Peripheral vision is usually preserved.

Getting a diagnosis

Detected via OCT scans, fundus photography, and fluorescein angiography. Annual sight tests with your optician can detect early AMD before symptoms appear.

Who it affects

Primarily affects people over 50. Smokers are up to four times more likely to develop AMD than non-smokers (Macular Society). Family history increases risk significantly. Most common cause of severe vision loss in the developed world.

Research & treatment progress

🌍 Available Abroad

Pegcetacoplan (Syfovre) — Geographic Atrophy

FDA-approved in the US (Feb 2023) as the first treatment for geographic atrophy. However, the UK's MHRA rejected the application in 2024-25, and the European Medicines Agency also issued a negative opinion. The NICE appraisal was suspended. Currently not available on the NHS or privately in the UK. Decisions cited the lack of demonstrated visual benefit despite slowing lesion growth.

✅ NHS Available

Faricimab (Vabysmo) — Wet AMD

Dual-pathway anti-VEGF injection targeting both VEGF and Ang-2. NICE approved. Fewer injections needed than older treatments — up to every 4 months for some patients.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

4D-150 Gene Therapy — 4FRONT Phase 3 Trials

Single injection delivering continuous anti-VEGF production — potentially replacing years of repeat injections. The 4FRONT-1 Phase 3 trial completed enrolment in February 2026 (~500 patients, ahead of schedule); topline data expected H1 2027. 4FRONT-2 (global) on track to complete enrolment H2 2026 with topline H2 2027.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

OCU410 (Ocugen) — ArMaDa Trial, Geographic Atrophy

Single subretinal injection targeting multiple disease pathways via RORA gene. Full Phase 2 12-month data (March 2026) showed 31% reduction in lesion growth in the optimal dose group versus control, plus 27% slower ellipsoid zone loss. No drug-related serious adverse events to date. Ocugen plans to initiate Phase 3 in 2026.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

PRIMA Retinal Implant (Science Corp) — Bionic Vision

A tiny chip placed under the retina combined with specialist glasses. The PRIMAvera trial in 38 people with geographic atrophy showed 81% had clinically meaningful improvement in visual acuity at 12 months — including reading letters they previously could not see. Results published in the New England Journal of Medicine (Oct 2025). Regulatory approval underway in Europe with expected market launch in 2026; FDA filing in progress.

Practical guides

Using Your Amsler Grid

Test each eye separately every day. If lines appear wavy, missing, or distorted — contact your eye clinic the same day. Wet AMD can progress within hours.

Understanding Anti-VEGF Injections

Anti-VEGF injections (Eylea, Lucentis, Vabysmo) are given at your eye clinic under local anaesthetic. They are highly effective at halting progression.

AREDS2 Supplements

For intermediate or advanced AMD, AREDS2 supplements (lutein, zeaxanthin, vitamins C and E, zinc and copper) were shown in large trials to slow AMD progressing further — by around a quarter. Use the AREDS2 formula, not the older beta-carotene version, which raised lung-cancer risk in people who smoke or used to. They don't help everyone, don't prevent AMD and don't restore lost vision — ask your ophthalmologist whether they're right for you.

Helpful technology

Smart Glasses

Ray-Ban Meta / Oakley Meta + Be My Eyes

Mainstream AI smart glasses with a built-in camera that can describe surroundings, read text aloud, and connect hands-free to a Be My Eyes volunteer or trusted contact by voice. They look like ordinary glasses and cost far less than dedicated low-vision wearables. Two generations are sold: Gen 1 is the cheaper option; Gen 2 adds longer battery (about 8 hours vs 4) and sharper video, but both get the same AI and Be My Eyes features. Oakley Meta is the sportier wraparound version.

Smart Glasses

NuEyes (wearable magnifier)

Lightweight voice-controlled wearable magnifier (up to 12×) with OCR text-to-speech, particularly useful when central vision is reduced. UK supplier is VisionAid Technologies, who can advise on the current NuEyes model.

AI Wearable

OrCam MyEye (reading & recognition camera)

Small camera that clips to any glasses frame and reads printed and digital text aloud. Current models also recognise faces, products, banknotes and colour; ask the supplier which model suits your needs.

CCTV Magnifier

Desktop Video Magnifier

Magnifies text and images on a large screen with adjustable contrast and colour modes; larger units add OCR to read text aloud. Smaller portable models are cheaper than full desktop systems.

Free App

Seeing AI (Microsoft)

Free iOS and Android app that describes scenes, reads printed and handwritten text, and identifies people, products, barcodes, and currency through your phone camera.

Free App

Be My Eyes

Free app that connects you by live video to sighted volunteers or company support agents who can describe what your camera is seeing.

Free App

Amsler Grid App

Benefits & registration

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

Request from your consultant ophthalmologist. Free. Unlocks local authority rehabilitation and council tax reduction.

Attendance Allowance

If over state pension age, Attendance Allowance pays £114.60/week (higher rate) from April 2026. Age UK can help complete the form.

Blue Badge

People with severe visual impairment automatically qualify for a Blue Badge. Apply via your local council.

Living with Age-Related Macular Degeneration

Daily Amsler Grid Monitoring

Test each eye separately every morning before getting up. Keep the grid at reading distance, cover one eye, and look at the central dot. Any new distortion, waviness, or missing areas requires same-day contact with your eye clinic — wet AMD can progress within hours.

Magnification at Home

A desktop video magnifier (CCTV) transforms reading, writing, and looking at photographs for people with AMD. Available free via NHS low vision clinic — ask your ophthalmologist for a referral. Handheld electronic magnifiers are also available.

Managing Central Vision Loss

Eccentric viewing — looking slightly to one side of what you want to see — uses the peripheral vision that AMD preserves. A vision rehabilitation specialist can train this technique, which significantly improves functional vision for many people.

Emotional Impact

AMD involves the loss of the vision used for faces, reading, and television — the things that make daily life rich. This is a real grief. RNIB Sight Loss Counselling (0303 123 9999) and the Macular Society's befriending service both provide free emotional support from people who understand.

Staying Connected

Large-print books are available free from RNIB. Audiobooks via RNIB Talking Books, Calibre Audio, and Audible keep reading accessible. Smart speakers (Amazon Echo, Google Home) provide hands-free access to news, music, timers, and calls.
