What is Myopic Macular Degeneration?

Myopic Macular Degeneration (MMD) is a serious complication of high myopia (severe short-sightedness, typically -6 dioptres or more) in which the elongated eyeball causes progressive damage to the macula. It is the leading cause of vision impairment in young and middle-aged adults in East Asia and is increasingly common in the UK. Unlike age-related AMD, MMD affects younger people and is directly caused by the physical stretching of the eye.

How it affects vision

Central vision progressively deteriorates. Straight lines may appear distorted or wavy. A blind spot may develop in the centre of vision. In some cases, abnormal blood vessels grow under the macula (myopic CNV — choroidal neovascularisation), causing rapid vision loss requiring urgent treatment. Peripheral vision is usually preserved.

Getting a diagnosis

OCT scanning identifies macular changes and choroidal thinning. Fluorescein angiography detects abnormal blood vessel growth. Regular monitoring by a retinal specialist is essential for people with high myopia. An Amsler grid used daily at home can detect early changes.

Who it affects

People with high myopia (usually -6 dioptres or more). Prevalence is rising globally due to increasing rates of myopia. More common in people of East Asian heritage. Can affect people from their 30s onwards, much earlier than age-related AMD.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Anti-VEGF for Myopic CNV (Urgent Treatment)

Ranibizumab (Lucentis) and aflibercept (Eylea) are approved for myopic CNV. Fewer injections than wet AMD — often only 1–3. Critical: seek same-day treatment if CNV is detected — rapid treatment preserves the most vision.

🔬 Pipeline

Posterior Scleral Reinforcement Surgery

A surgical procedure physically supporting the back of the elongated eye to slow further stretching and prevent MMD progression. Being investigated in several countries. Could be the first treatment targeting the physical cause of MMD rather than its complications.

🔬 Pipeline

Myopia Control in Children

Slowing myopia progression in childhood may reduce the risk of high myopia and later MMD. In late 2025 the MHRA licensed the first low-dose atropine eye drop in the UK (Ryjunea, for children aged 3–14 with progressing myopia), which became available during 2026. Important: it is currently a private prescription only and is not funded by the NHS — a NICE appraisal is still awaited. A UK trial published in the BMJ in 2026 found the drops were safe but the benefit was small, reducing myopia progression by about 0.38 dioptres over two years. Orthokeratology (specially shaped contact lenses worn overnight) and myopia-control spectacles are also used, again usually privately. Discuss the costs, evidence and limitations with an optometrist or paediatric ophthalmologist.

Practical guides

Monitor Daily with an Amsler Grid

If you have high myopia, test each eye separately every day with an Amsler grid. If any lines appear wavy, distorted, or missing, contact your eye clinic the same day — myopic CNV can progress within hours and early treatment is critical.

Get Regular Specialist Review

Ask your optometrist for referral to a medical retina specialist if you have high myopia. Annual OCT scans can detect macular changes before symptoms appear.

Protect Your Eyes from Impact

High myopes have a higher risk of retinal detachment. Avoid contact sports without eye protection. Sudden flashing lights or a curtain across your vision requires immediate A&E attendance.

Register and Claim

MMD causing significant vision loss supports a CVI application and PIP claim. Ask your ophthalmologist and call RNIB (0303 123 9999) for guidance.

Helpful technology

Monitoring

Amsler Grid App

A free daily self-monitoring app (or printed grid) to catch new distortion early — important for spotting myopic CNV (new blood vessel growth) before it causes lasting damage. Report any new wavy or missing patches to your eye clinic urgently.

Assessment

NHS Low-Vision Assessment

A free NHS low-vision assessment matches you to the right magnifiers, lighting and tinted lenses for your vision, and often lends optical aids at no cost. Ask your ophthalmologist or optometrist for a referral.

Magnification

Magnifiers (Optical, Handheld & Desktop)

For the central reading difficulty, optical aids may be loaned free via the low-vision clinic; electronic handheld magnifiers add contrast and colour, and desktop CCTV magnifiers suit sustained reading.

AI Wearable

OrCam / Ray-Ban Meta + Be My Eyes

OrCam MyEye Smart clips to glasses and reads text and recognises faces/products; mainstream Ray-Ban Meta glasses with Be My Eyes describe surroundings and connect to a sighted helper at lower cost.

Free App

Seeing AI / Be My Eyes

Read text and identify people, products and currency (Seeing AI), or get live help from a sighted volunteer (Be My Eyes). Both free, iOS and Android.

Benefits & registration

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

If MMD has significantly affected your vision, ask your ophthalmologist for a CVI. Free and unlocks rehabilitation and financial support.

PIP

Central vision loss from MMD supports a PIP claim. RNIB can help with the form — 0303 123 9999.

Free NHS Eye Tests

People with sight impairment certificates receive free NHS eye tests. Register with your optician.
