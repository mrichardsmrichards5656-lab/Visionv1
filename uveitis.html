Uveitis

What is Uveitis?

Uveitis, sometimes called iritis, is inflammation inside part of the eye. It is rare. It usually affects one eye, though both can be affected. It needs prompt treatment because, if untreated, it can cause long-term sight loss and even blindness.

How it affects vision

Symptoms can include eye pain (often worse when reading), red or watery eyes, reduced or blurred vision, sensitivity to light, and flashes or floaters. They can appear within hours or days, or develop over weeks or months. Sometimes there are no symptoms. Get an urgent GP appointment or call NHS 111 if you have eye pain, light sensitivity, changes in vision or a very red eye. Go to A&E or call 999 if you lose any vision.

Getting a diagnosis

A GP will refer you to an ophthalmologist, who is usually seen within 24 hours. The specialist will examine your eye and look for a cause.

Who it affects

The cause is often unknown. Known triggers include autoimmune conditions (such as multiple sclerosis and ankylosing spondylitis), infections (herpes simplex, shingles and toxoplasmosis), eye injury and, rarely, a growth in the eye.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Treatment to Clear Infection and Calm Inflammation

Treatment is usually a combination of medicines to clear any infection, reduce inflammation, and relax the eye and ease pain. The aim is to prevent long-term sight loss. Uveitis usually improves quickly with treatment but can come back, so follow-up checks matter.

✅ NHS Available

Stronger Treatment for Severe or Recurring Uveitis

If uveitis is severe or keeps returning, specialists may use immunosuppressant medicines or TNF inhibitors, laser phototherapy, cryotherapy, or eye surgery. Follow-up checks look at how well treatment is working, side effects and complications.

Practical guides

Treat It as Urgent

Eye pain, light sensitivity, blurred vision, flashes or floaters, or a very red eye need an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111. Any loss of vision needs A&E or 999. Early treatment is the best protection for your sight.

Keep Your Follow-Up Appointments

Uveitis can return, and complications can develop without obvious symptoms. Follow-up checks look at how treatment is working and whether there are side effects.

Take Medicines as Prescribed

Do not stop eye drops or tablets early or change the dose without advice from your eye team, even if the eye feels better.

Ask About the Cause

Uveitis can be linked to autoimmune conditions or infections. Ask your eye specialist whether tests are needed, and tell any new doctor that you have had uveitis.

Do Not Wait if Symptoms Return

Uveitis can come back. If pain, redness or blurred vision return, do not wait for your next appointment: contact your eye clinic, GP or NHS 111 promptly.

Helpful technology

Screen Settings

Dark Mode, Contrast & Zoom

Larger text, high contrast, dark mode and screen zoom are built into all phones, tablets and computers and can reduce glare and strain at no cost.

Magnification

Magnifiers (Optical, Handheld & Desktop)

Optical magnifiers may be loaned free via an NHS low-vision clinic; electronic handheld magnifiers and larger desktop video magnifiers offer more power for longer reading.

Free App

Seeing AI and Be My Eyes

Two free smartphone apps. Seeing AI uses the phone camera to read text aloud and describe products and scenes; Be My Eyes connects you with a sighted volunteer by video for help with a task.

Benefits & registration

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

Ask your consultant ophthalmologist about a Certificate of Vision Impairment, which is free. Registration can open access to local authority rehabilitation and support, free NHS sight tests and travel concessions. A council tax reduction is sometimes possible but is not automatic, so ask your council. Whether you qualify depends on how much sight you have, and your consultant decides. Uveitis can sometimes cause lasting sight loss; if it has, ask your eye clinic.

Personal Independence Payment (PIP)

Awards depend on how your sight affects daily life, not on the diagnosis. Apply at gov.uk/pip or call 0800 917 2222. In Scotland, Adult Disability Payment replaces PIP — apply through Social Security Scotland on 0800 182 2222. RNIB can help with the form on 0303 123 9999.

Access to Work

A government grant towards workplace adjustments and specialist equipment. Apply at gov.uk/access-to-work. There is currently a long waiting list, so apply as early as you can.

Living with Uveitis

Light Sensitivity

Light sensitivity is common during flare-ups. Dimmer indoor lighting and dark mode on screens may be more comfortable; ask your optometrist about tinted lenses.

Driving

You must meet the legal eyesight standard to drive, and you must tell the DVLA if your eyes do not meet it. If your vision is blurred or your eyes are painful or light-sensitive, do not drive. Ask your eye clinic if you are unsure.

Work and Study

Screen magnification, good non-glare lighting and adjusted monitor positioning are reasonable adjustments that employers must consider. Access to Work can help fund specialist equipment, though waits are long, so apply early.
