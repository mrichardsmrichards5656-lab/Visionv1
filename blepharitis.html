What is Blepharitis?

Blepharitis is a common chronic inflammation of the eyelids, affecting the eyelid margins where the eyelashes grow. It is one of the most common eye conditions in the UK, affecting people of all ages. There are two main types: anterior blepharitis (affecting the outer eyelid margin, often associated with bacteria or skin conditions such as seborrhoeic dermatitis or rosacea) and posterior blepharitis (affecting the inner eyelid margin, caused by dysfunction of the meibomian glands which produce the oily layer of the tear film). Many people have both types.

How it affects vision

Sore, red, itchy, and swollen eyelids. Crusty or sticky eyelashes, particularly in the morning. Burning or gritty sensation in the eyes. Dry eyes caused by disruption of the tear film. Sensitivity to light. Blurred vision that improves with blinking. In most cases blepharitis does not permanently damage vision, but it can cause significant discomfort and affect quality of life. In severe cases it can lead to corneal damage.

Getting a diagnosis

Diagnosed by an optometrist or ophthalmologist through examination of the eyelid margins under a slit lamp. Meibomian gland expression and photography can assess gland function. No specific tests are needed for a diagnosis.

Who it affects

Extremely common — affects millions of people in the UK. More common with age. Associated with skin conditions including rosacea, seborrhoeic dermatitis, and acne. Contact lens wearers are at higher risk. Blepharitis is chronic — it rarely disappears completely but can be very well controlled with daily lid hygiene.

Research & treatment progress

🌍 Available Privately

Intense Pulsed Light (IPL) Therapy

IPL therapy can help posterior blepharitis caused by meibomian gland dysfunction. Evidence shows it reduces dry-eye symptoms compared with no treatment, though its added benefit on top of standard lid hygiene is less certain. Available privately at specialist UK eye clinics — typically 4 sessions over 3 months. Not NHS-funded; private cost roughly £300–600 per course.

🌍 Available Abroad

Lotilaner (Xdemvy) — Demodex Treatment

FDA approved July 2023 in the US for Demodex mite-associated blepharitis. Demodex is an increasingly recognised cause — more common than previously thought, accounting for up to 45% of blepharitis cases. No MHRA approval announced for the UK as of 2026. Daily lid hygiene remains the standard UK treatment.

✅ NHS Available

Daily Lid Hygiene — The Most Important Treatment

Warm compress + lid cleaning every day is proven to significantly reduce symptoms and is the foundation of all blepharitis management. Consistency matters more than any medication. A heated eye mask (Bruder, TheraEye) is significantly more effective than a flannel.

Practical guides

Daily Lid Hygiene — The Most Important Thing

Warm compress for 2 minutes (a heated eye mask works well — Bruder mask recommended), then gently clean the eyelid margins with a lint-free pad and dedicated lid wipes (Blephasol, Blephaclean, or Sterilid). The NHS still mentions diluted baby shampoo as an option, but dedicated lid wipes are now generally preferred as they don't contain detergents that can irritate the tear film. Do this every day — consistency is everything with blepharitis.

Warm Compresses Done Properly

The compress needs to be warm enough (around 40°C) and held for long enough (at least 2 minutes) to melt the thickened oil in the meibomian glands. A microwaveable heated eye mask (Bruder, TheraEye) maintains temperature much better than a flannel.

Omega-3 Supplements

Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil or flaxseed oil, 2–3g daily) improve meibomian gland function and reduce symptoms in most people with posterior blepharitis. Allow 3 months for full effect. Ask your ophthalmologist before starting.

Dry Eye Management

Preservative-free artificial tears (Hylo-Tear, Systane Ultra) used regularly throughout the day manage dry eye symptoms. Preservative-free drops are important for long-term use — preservatives in standard drops can worsen blepharitis over time.

Helpful technology

Treatment

Heated Eye Mask (Bruder, TheraPearl)

A reusable microwaveable mask holds the right temperature (around 40°C) for long enough to melt the thickened oil in the eyelid glands — far more effective than a flannel that cools quickly. Use daily for posterior blepharitis.

Lid Hygiene

Lid-Cleaning Wipes & Foams

Pre-made lid wipes or foams (e.g. Blephaclean, Blephasol, OcuSoft) clean the lash line gently and consistently, which is the cornerstone of controlling blepharitis. Cheaper than repeatedly buying drops and available from pharmacies.

Drops

Preservative-Free Artificial Tears

Drops such as Hylo-Tear, Systane Ultra PF or Evolve ease the dry-eye symptoms blepharitis causes. Preservative-free is important for frequent long-term use, as preservatives can worsen the condition.

Note

Vision Aids Rarely Needed

Blepharitis affects the eyelids and tear film, not the retina, so it seldom causes lasting low vision — the focus is comfort and control. If your vision is affected, ask your optometrist, as another cause may be involved.

Benefits & registration

Free NHS Eye Tests

If blepharitis has affected your vision or you have a related condition, check whether you qualify for free NHS eye tests via your optician.

Prescription Prepayment Certificate

If you are prescribed treatments for blepharitis regularly, a Prescription Prepayment Certificate (PPC) caps your costs at around £32 for 3 months of unlimited prescriptions.

PIP if Severely Affected

Severe blepharitis causing significant vision problems or limiting daily activities may support a PIP claim. Contact RNIB (0303 123 9999) for advice.
