Best Apps for Blind & Partially Sighted People

A clear, up-to-date guide to the apps that help most with sight loss — grouped by what they do. Most are free, and several are already built into your phone.

Last checked: July 2026. Apps change often — they can be discontinued, renamed, or move from free to paid. Always check the price and reviews in your app store before downloading.

Read text aloud (camera apps)

Point your phone's camera at text — a letter, a label, a menu — and these apps read it aloud. They can also describe scenes, identify products and tell you colours.

iPhone & Android · Free

Seeing AI

Reads printed and handwritten text, identifies products, money and colours, and describes scenes and people. One of the most popular free starting points.

App Store ↗   Google Play ↗

iPhone & Android · Free + optional paid upgrade

Envision AI

Reads text, recognises faces, describes surroundings, and includes an AI assistant called Ally. Supports many languages.

Download ↗

Android · Free

Google Lookout

Reads text, labels and currency and describes objects. Designed for Android, with strong integration on Pixel and recent devices.

Google Play ↗

Live human help

Sometimes the quickest answer is another person. These apps connect you, by live video, to someone who can see what your camera sees.

iPhone & Android · Free

Be My Eyes

Connects you by live video to a sighted volunteer for everyday tasks, and includes a built-in AI photo-describer. Free, with a very large volunteer community.

App Store ↗   Google Play ↗

iPhone & Android · Paid (some free time)

Aira

Connects you to professionally trained agents for more complex tasks like navigating an airport or filling in a form. Paid, with a short amount of free time each day.

App Store ↗   Google Play ↗

Getting around (navigation)

These apps build awareness of your surroundings using audio. They are aids to confidence, not a replacement for orientation and mobility training.

iPhone · Free

VoiceVista / Soundscape

Uses 3D audio cues to describe your surroundings as you move, helping you build a mental map. Free versions include VoiceVista and the community Soundscape apps.

App Store ↗

iPhone · Paid (one-off)

BlindSquare

Describes points of interest and intersections as you travel. Developed in collaboration with blind users.

App Store ↗

iPhone & Android · Free

Lazarillo

Announces nearby places, streets and intersections as you walk, like a running commentary on your surroundings.

Download ↗

Books and newspapers

Free accessible reading, including the UK's most popular talking-book service.

iPhone & Android · Free

Dolphin EasyReader

Gives access to RNIB Talking Books, newspapers and over 40 accessible libraries worldwide. Text and audio are synced, with adjustable fonts and colours. Free RNIB Reading Services account required.

App Store ↗   Google Play ↗

Alexa smart speaker · Free with RNIB membership

RNIB Talking Books on Alexa

Listen to Talking Books on an Amazon Alexa smart speaker, which even remembers where you got to in each book. Free with RNIB membership.

Identifying money and products

Quick checks for everyday independence.

iPhone & Android · Free basic + paid upgrade

Cash Reader

Identifies banknotes by sound or vibration, works offline, and supports many currencies including pounds.

Download ↗

iPhone & Android · Free

Seeing AI, Envision & Lookout

The camera apps listed above also identify products, scan barcodes and tell you colours — useful for shopping, cooking and sorting post.

Already built into your phone (free)

Before downloading anything, it is worth switching on the accessibility tools your phone already has. They cost nothing and are often all someone needs.

iPhone / Android · Free, built in

VoiceOver (iPhone) & TalkBack (Android)

Read aloud everything on the screen so you can use the phone by touch and sound. Turn on via Settings then Accessibility.

iPhone / Android · Free, built in

Magnifier & Large Text

Turn the phone into a magnifying glass and enlarge all on-screen text. Built into both iPhone and Android, no download needed.

Not sure where to start?

If you only try one, Seeing AI (for reading) or Be My Eyes (for human help) are free, popular first downloads. For patient, free help choosing and learning any of these, contact the organisations below.
