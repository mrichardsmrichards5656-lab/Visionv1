What is Thyroid Eye Disease?

Thyroid eye disease (TED), also called Graves' orbitopathy, is an autoimmune inflammatory disorder that affects the tissues around the eye. It is mainly seen in people with an overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism) caused by Graves' disease. About one in four people with Graves' disease develop eye problems. The British Thyroid Foundation estimates that around 50,000 people in the UK are affected.

How it affects vision

Symptoms can include staring or bulging eyes, redness and swelling of the eyes and lids, grittiness, dryness, watery eyes, sensitivity to bright light, swollen or full-feeling eyelids, blurred or double vision, and pain in or behind the eye. Symptoms can be mistaken for allergy, hay fever or conjunctivitis. See a doctor without delay and ask for an immediate referral to a specialist eye centre if symptoms steadily worsen over days or weeks, blurred vision does not improve with blinking or covering each eye in turn, colours look less bright or differ between your eyes, you have double vision looking forward or downward, or you need to tilt your head to avoid double vision.

Getting a diagnosis

Care should involve an ophthalmologist (eye specialist) and an endocrinologist (thyroid specialist), ideally at a specialist eye centre with regular TED experience. Early diagnosis is essential for treatment to be effective. Ask your GP to refer you if you have thyroid problems and eye symptoms.

Who it affects

TED mainly affects people with Graves' disease, an autoimmune thyroid condition. Smoking is the most important risk you can change: smokers have roughly double the risk of developing TED, heavy smokers about eight times the risk, and smokers respond less well to treatment. Quitting helps, and there is no research yet on vaping and TED.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Artificial Tears and Selenium

Artificial tears help with mild dryness and irritation. For mild active TED, the British Thyroid Foundation says a six-month course of selenium (100mcg twice daily) may help. Ask your specialist before starting any supplement.

✅ NHS Available

Steroids, Radiotherapy and Surgery

Steroids reduce inflammation, double vision, redness and swelling; given through a vein they are more effective, with fewer side effects, than tablets. Radiotherapy may be combined with steroids or immunosuppressants. Once inflammation has settled, surgery can include orbital decompression, eye muscle surgery for double vision, and eyelid surgery. Radioiodine should be avoided during active TED as it can worsen it.

🌅 Horizon

Newer Biologic Treatments

Newer biologic medicines for TED exist but are costly, not widely available and carry side effects. NICE has an appraisal under way for one of them, teprotumumab, but it is not routinely available on the NHS. Ask your specialist about the current position.

Practical guides

Know the Urgent Warning Signs

Steadily worsening symptoms, blurred vision that doesn't clear with blinking, duller colours, double vision looking forward or down, or needing to tilt your head all need an immediate referral to a specialist eye centre. Early treatment protects sight.

Stop Smoking

The British Thyroid Foundation calls this the most important step. Smoking raises the risk and reduces how well treatment works. Ask your GP or a stop-smoking service for help; the benefit seems to build after quitting.

Ask for a Joint Clinic

Look for a specialist eye centre that sees TED regularly and works with an endocrinologist. The British Thyroid Foundation and BOPSS list joint clinics.

Keep Your Thyroid Under Control

Ask your endocrinologist how your thyroid treatment affects your eyes. Avoid radioiodine during active TED unless your specialists advise otherwise.

Look After Your Eyes Day to Day

Artificial tears help with mild dryness and irritation. Ask your specialist about other ways to ease symptoms.

Helpful technology

Screen Settings

Dark Mode, Contrast & Zoom

Larger text, high contrast, dark mode and screen zoom are built into all phones, tablets and computers and can reduce glare and strain at no cost.

Free App

Seeing AI and Be My Eyes

Two free smartphone apps. Seeing AI uses the phone camera to read text aloud and describe products and scenes; Be My Eyes connects you with a sighted volunteer by video for help with a task.

Magnification

Magnifiers (Optical, Handheld & Desktop)

Optical magnifiers may be loaned free via an NHS low-vision clinic; electronic handheld magnifiers and larger desktop video magnifiers offer more power for longer reading.

Benefits & registration

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

Ask your consultant ophthalmologist about a Certificate of Vision Impairment, which is free. Registration can open access to local authority rehabilitation and support, free NHS sight tests and travel concessions. A council tax reduction is sometimes possible but is not automatic, so ask your council. Whether you qualify depends on how much sight you have, and your consultant decides. TED can cause lasting sight loss in some people; if so, ask your consultant.

Personal Independence Payment (PIP)

Awards depend on how your sight affects daily life, not on the diagnosis. Apply at gov.uk/pip or call 0800 917 2222. In Scotland, Adult Disability Payment replaces PIP — apply through Social Security Scotland on 0800 182 2222. RNIB can help with the form on 0303 123 9999.

Access to Work

A government grant towards workplace adjustments and specialist equipment. Apply at gov.uk/access-to-work. There is currently a long waiting list, so apply as early as you can.

Living with Thyroid Eye Disease

Changes in Appearance

TED can change how your eyes look, which can affect confidence. Eyelid surgery and other treatments may help once the condition settles. The British Thyroid Foundation offers wellbeing resources and patient stories.

Double Vision and Driving

Double vision can make driving unsafe, and the law requires you to tell the DVLA if your eyesight does not meet the standard. Do not drive with double vision. Ask your eye specialist for advice.

Work and Study

Screen magnification, good non-glare lighting and adjusted monitor positioning are reasonable adjustments that employers must consider. Access to Work can help fund specialist equipment, though waits are long, so apply early.
