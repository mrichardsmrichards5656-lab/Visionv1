What are Optic Nerve Hypoplasia and Optic Atrophy?

The optic nerve carries signals from the eye to the brain. In optic nerve hypoplasia (ONH) the nerve is underdeveloped from birth, in one eye or both. Optic atrophy means nerve fibres in the optic nerve have been lost; it can happen at any age and has many causes, including inherited conditions such as autosomal dominant optic atrophy (ADOA). ONH is thought to be one of the three most common causes of visual impairment in children. When ONH comes with pituitary hormone problems or differences in the middle of the brain, it is called septo-optic dysplasia.

How it affects vision

With ONH, vision ranges from almost normal, especially if only one eye is mildly affected, to seeing only large objects or bright light. There may be nystagmus (wobbling eyes), mild light sensitivity, reduced side vision and poor depth perception. ONH does not get worse: vision that develops is not later lost because of it. With ADOA, sight slowly and painlessly worsens in both eyes, usually from childhood or the teenage years, and colours become harder to tell apart. In septo-optic dysplasia, hormone problems can affect growth, blood sugar and how the body copes with illness.

Getting a diagnosis

An eye doctor examines the head of the optic nerve at the back of the eye, which can be hard to judge in a very young baby. An MRI brain scan can show the size of the optic nerves and the pituitary gland, and electrical tests of the visual pathway (visual evoked potentials) can help estimate a young child's vision. Blood tests check hormone levels where septo-optic dysplasia is suspected. For inherited optic atrophy, genetic testing is free on the NHS when you are referred by an ophthalmologist or GP.

Who it affects

ONH affects boys and girls. In most cases the cause is unknown, it is very rarely inherited, and no other family member is affected. Septo-optic dysplasia affects around 1 in every 10,000 births. With ADOA, each child of an affected parent has a 1 in 2 chance of inheriting it, and about 75 in 100 cases are linked to a gene called OPA1. Sight can vary a lot even within one family.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Hormone Checks and Replacement

Children with ONH may be checked for septo-optic dysplasia. If the pituitary gland does not make enough of some hormones, a team including an endocrinologist replaces them. Low cortisol can be life-threatening during severe illness or after an accident, so ask your team what to do when your child is unwell.

✅ NHS Available

Supporting Vision as It Develops

There is no cure for ONH, and treatment addresses each child's difficulties. Vision may develop over the first couple of years, and play with bright toys and lights may help a baby learn to fix and follow. Children's needs should be reassessed as they grow.

✅ NHS Available

Genetic Testing and Counselling (Inherited Optic Atrophy)

A blood test can identify the gene change, clarify how the condition is inherited and give some idea of how sight may change. Testing and genetic counselling are free on the NHS. There is currently no treatment to stop or reverse sight loss from ADOA; research into treatments is at an early stage.

Practical guides

Ask About Hormone Checks

If your child has optic nerve hypoplasia, ask whether hormone tests or a referral to a children's hormone specialist (endocrinologist) are needed. Tell your doctor about poor growth, low blood sugar or frequent infections. If hormones are being replaced, ask for clear written instructions for illness and emergencies.

Encourage Looking in the Early Years

Vision may keep developing over the first couple of years. Bright toys, lights and high-contrast objects, and books and toys of a size your child can see, can help. Your child can still learn to climb stairs and use slides with teaching and time.

Mention Sleep Problems

Children with severe visual impairment can have disrupted sleep. Tell your paediatrician or health visitor, as there are ways to help.

Ask About Support at Nursery and School

Ask your local authority's vision impairment (sensory) service about an assessment. A Qualified Teacher of Children and Young People with Vision Impairment (QTVI) may be able to advise the nursery or school. Support is assessed and varies between areas, so ask early. Contact's free helpline for families of disabled children is 0808 808 3555.

Tell Relatives About Inherited Optic Atrophy

If you have ADOA, close relatives may wish to have their eyes checked. Genetic counselling on the NHS can explain the chances for children and help with family planning. About 1 in 5 people with ADOA have other symptoms too, most often hearing loss starting in the twenties or thirties, so mention any change in hearing.

Ask for a Low Vision Assessment

If your sight stays reduced, ask your ophthalmologist or optometrist for a referral to an NHS low vision service — the assessment is free. They work from what you actually struggle with (reading, screens, faces, getting about) and match aids and lighting to it. Optical magnifiers are normally loaned free for as long as they help; electronic and desktop video magnifiers are usually not funded.

Benefits & registration

Disability Living Allowance for Children

A child under 16 who needs more care or supervision than other children of the same age because of their sight may qualify for Disability Living Allowance. It is assessed on your child's needs, not the diagnosis, and a decision can be challenged. Apply at gov.uk/disability-living-allowance-children. In Scotland, Child Disability Payment replaces it: apply through Social Security Scotland on 0800 182 2222.

Personal Independence Payment (PIP)

Awards depend on how your sight affects daily life, not on the diagnosis. Apply at gov.uk/pip or call 0800 917 2222. In Scotland, Adult Disability Payment replaces PIP — apply through Social Security Scotland on 0800 182 2222. RNIB can help with the form on 0303 123 9999.

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

Ask your consultant ophthalmologist about a Certificate of Vision Impairment, which is free. Registration can open access to local authority rehabilitation and support, free NHS sight tests and travel concessions. A council tax reduction is sometimes possible but is not automatic, so ask your council. Whether you qualify depends on how much sight you have, and your consultant decides.

Free NHS Sight Tests

Sight tests are free on the NHS if you are aged 60 or over (throughout the UK), and for everyone in Scotland. They are also free for people with diabetes or glaucoma, people registered as sight impaired, and some others, including children under 16. Ask your optician.

Living with Optic Nerve Hypoplasia and Optic Atrophy

Nursery and School

Tell the nursery or school about your child's sight early. Large print, good lighting, sitting near the front and extra time are common adjustments. Extra support is assessed by the school and local authority rather than automatic, and you can ask for an assessment.

Driving

Reduced central or side vision from an optic nerve condition can affect whether you can drive. You must be able to meet the eyesight standard for driving, so do not drive until you have been told it is safe, and ask your doctor or optician about telling the DVLA. In Northern Ireland the DVA applies.

Work and Study

Screen magnification, good non-glare lighting and adjusted monitor positioning are reasonable adjustments that employers must consider. Access to Work can help fund specialist equipment, though waits are long, so apply early.

For Parents — Optic Nerve Hypoplasia

A diagnosis of optic nerve hypoplasia often comes very early, when it is hard to know how much your baby will see. Vision can keep developing over the first couple of years, and the condition itself does not get worse.

It Does Not Get Worse

ONH is non-progressive. Vision your child develops is not later lost because of ONH.

Hormones Matter Too

Some children also have pituitary hormone problems (septo-optic dysplasia). Only about one-third of children with septo-optic dysplasia have all three of its features, so ask what has been checked.

It Is Very Rarely Inherited

Most cases happen with no family history. Tell your obstetrician in a future pregnancy that a previous child has ONH.
