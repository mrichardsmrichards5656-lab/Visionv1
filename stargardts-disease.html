Stargardt's Disease

What is Stargardt's Disease?

Stargardt disease is the most common inherited macular dystrophy, affecting around 1 in 8,000–10,000 people. It is caused by mutations in the ABCA4 gene, leading to a toxic build-up of lipofuscin in the retinal pigment epithelium. Central vision progressively deteriorates, often starting in childhood or young adulthood. Peripheral vision is usually retained.

How it affects vision

Central vision progressively deteriorates, often starting in childhood or young adulthood. Peripheral vision is usually retained. Common symptoms include blurry central vision, difficulty reading, and sensitivity to bright light.

Getting a diagnosis

Typically diagnosed via fundus photography (showing yellow-white flecks), fluorescein angiography, electroretinography (ERG), and genetic testing. A retinal specialist or inherited eye disease clinic can confirm diagnosis.

Who it affects

Affects approximately 1 in 8,000–10,000 people. Usually appears between ages 6–20, though adult-onset forms exist. Equal prevalence across sexes.

Research & treatment progress

🔬 Pipeline

Tinlarebant (Belite Bio) — FDA Rolling Submission in Progress

Oral tablet taken daily. Slowed lesion growth by 35.7% in the Phase 3 DRAGON trial in adolescents. Belite Bio began a rolling NDA submission to the FDA on 21 April 2026, expected to complete in Q2 2026. Regulatory applications also accepted in UK and China based on interim data. Granted FDA Breakthrough Therapy, Fast Track, and Rare Pediatric Disease designations. Not yet approved or available. Could become the first approved treatment for Stargardt disease worldwide.

🔬 Pipeline

SpliceBio SB-007 — ASTRA Trial

Protein splicing therapy delivered by single subretinal injection. Now in dose-expansion phase (Jan 2026). One-time treatment designed to deliver a functional ABCA4 gene across all ABCA4 mutations. Raised $135M in 2025 to advance the programme.

🔬 Pipeline

AAVantgarde AAVB-039 — CELESTE Trial (UK Recruiting)

Dual-vector AAV gene therapy cleared by the UK MHRA for clinical trial in Oct 2025. FDA Orphan Drug designation and Fast Track status. Recruiting in US and UK. Contact AAVantgarde directly to enquire about eligibility.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

Ascidian ACDN-01 — STELLAR Trial (Phase 1/2)

A novel approach that rewrites RNA rather than editing DNA. Unlike gene replacement, it corrects the genetic message without permanent DNA changes. The Phase 1/2 STELLAR trial completed adult dose escalation in May 2026 and has expanded to enrolling adolescents aged 12 and older. First-in-human safety data presented at the American Society of Gene & Cell Therapy meeting in May 2026. Potentially applicable to patients with a wide range of ABCA4 mutations.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

Ocugen OCU410ST — GARDian3 Trial (Phase 2/3 — Enrolment Complete)

AAV-based modifier gene therapy targeting multiple disease pathways simultaneously — potentially applicable to patients with a wider range of ABCA4 mutations than single-gene approaches. Enrolment completed in April 2026 (63 participants in under 9 months). Interim 8-month data expected mid-2026; topline 1-year results expected Q2 2027; BLA filing targeted mid-2027. Positive Phase 1 GARDian1 results published in Nature Eye in January 2026.

🔬 Pipeline

Stem Cell RPE Replacement

Several programmes replacing lost retinal pigment epithelium cells with lab-grown stem cell-derived cells. Phase 1/2 data shows long-term safety at 3 years and early vision improvements.

Practical guides

Getting a Referral to a Retinal Specialist

Ask your GP or optometrist for an urgent referral to an ophthalmologist specialising in inherited retinal diseases. In the UK, ask for a Medical Retina or Inherited Eye Disease clinic at your nearest teaching hospital.

Registering as Sight Impaired

Your consultant ophthalmologist can issue a Certificate of Visual Impairment (CVI), enabling registration with your local authority and unlocking social services, rehabilitation, and council tax reductions.

Setting Up Accessibility on Your Phone

On iPhone: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size. On Android: Settings → Accessibility → Vision. Enable large text, bold text, and display zoom. VoiceOver (iPhone) and TalkBack (Android) read everything aloud.

Applying for Disability Benefits

In the UK, you may be eligible for PIP or DLA (children under 16). Apply at gov.uk/pip or call 0800 917 2222. RNIB and Citizens Advice can help. Don't wait — claims can take months.

Protecting Remaining Vision

Wear wraparound UV-blocking sunglasses outdoors, and avoid smoking, which can accelerate progression.

Be Careful With Eye Supplements

If you have Stargardt's, avoid vitamin A and beta-carotene supplements — the Stargardt's retina can't process them properly and they may speed up the build-up that damages it. This includes the original AREDS formula (high in beta-carotene), so don't assume 'eye vitamins' are safe. Safe carotenoids such as lutein and zeaxanthin don't convert to vitamin A. Always check with your specialist before taking any supplement.

Benefits & registration

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

Ask your consultant ophthalmologist — it's free. Unlocks local authority rehabilitation, council tax reduction, free bus pass, and more.

Personal Independence Payment (PIP)

Apply at gov.uk/pip or call 0800 917 2222. Both Daily Living and Mobility components may apply. RNIB can help with the form.

Access to Work

Government grant funding workplace adjustments and specialist equipment. Apply at gov.uk/access-to-work.

Living with Stargardt's Disease

Reading and Screen Use

Use dark mode on all devices and increase font size significantly. Microsoft's Seeing AI reads any printed or digital text aloud via your phone camera — transforming reading with central vision loss. An eccentric viewing technique — looking slightly to the side of what you want to see — uses your preserved peripheral vision and can be trained with a vision rehabilitation specialist.

Lighting at Home

Bright, even task lighting reduces the effort of using peripheral vision. Avoid glare — position lights to the side rather than overhead. Dimmer switches allow adjustment as conditions change throughout the day.

Driving

Many people with Stargardt's cannot meet the legal visual standard for driving and must inform the DVLA. This is one of the most emotionally significant losses — RNIB's emotional support line (0303 123 9999) and peer groups understand this specifically. Mobility alternatives including Motability and taxi schemes are worth exploring immediately.

At Work

Request a workplace needs assessment. Screen magnification software (ZoomText), adjusted monitor positioning, and good task lighting are all reasonable adjustments under the Equality Act. Access to Work funds specialist equipment — apply before buying anything privately.

Sun Sensitivity

Wraparound UV-blocking sunglasses are essential outdoors. Some people with Stargardt's find light sensitivity significantly affects daily life — tinted lenses (amber or plum) reduce glare and improve comfort in bright conditions.

For Parents — Stargardt Disease

Stargardt disease is often diagnosed in childhood or teenage years. If your child has just been diagnosed, this section is for you.

Understanding the Diagnosis

Stargardt disease affects central vision but peripheral vision is usually preserved. Your child will be able to see — they will navigate the world using their peripheral vision, and with the right support, they will adapt remarkably well.

Getting the Right Support at School

Your child is entitled to large print materials, preferential seating, a tablet or laptop, and extra time in exams. Request a QTVI (Qualified Teacher of Visual Impairment) from your local authority immediately. The earlier the support is in place, the better.

Genetic Testing

Ask for a referral to an inherited retinal disease clinic for genetic testing. This is free on the NHS and determines eligibility for clinical trials — including gene therapy — which are progressing rapidly for Stargardt disease.

DLA — Apply Now

Your child is eligible for Disability Living Allowance. Call RNIB (0303 123 9999) for free help with the form. Do not delay — the application takes time and backdating is not available.

The Macular Society

The Macular Society has a dedicated service for children and young people with Stargardt disease and their families. Peer support from other families is invaluable — macularsociety.org or 0300 3030 111.
