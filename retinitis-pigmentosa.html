What is Retinitis Pigmentosa?

Retinitis Pigmentosa (RP) covers over 100 inherited retinal dystrophies affecting around 1 in 4,000 people. Rod photoreceptors are damaged first, affecting peripheral and night vision. It is the most common inherited cause of visual disability in working-age adults in the UK.

How it affects vision

Night blindness is usually the first symptom. Peripheral vision gradually narrows, creating tunnel vision. In later stages, central vision may also be affected. The rate of progression varies enormously between individuals.

Getting a diagnosis

Electroretinography (ERG), visual field testing, OCT scanning, fundus photography, and genetic testing. Genetic testing is strongly recommended as it determines treatment eligibility.

Who it affects

Affects approximately 1 in 4,000 people. Symptoms typically begin in the teenage years. Over 100 different genes can cause RP. X-linked forms tend to be more severe.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Luxturna — RPE65 Mutations

First gene therapy approved on the NHS for an inherited eye condition. A single subretinal injection in each eye. Available now at specialist centres for patients with biallelic RPE65 mutations. Life-changing results in eligible patients.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

Botaretigene Sparoparvovec (bota-vec) — RPGR Gene Therapy

The Phase 3 LUMEOS trial (95 patients) did not meet its primary endpoint of vision-guided mobility (May 2025), but showed meaningful improvements in visual acuity and patient-reported outcomes. Johnson & Johnson returned rights to MeiraGTx in April 2026; MeiraGTx plans regulatory filings in the US, Europe and Japan based on the existing data, with potential commercial launch in 2027 if approved. For males with X-linked RP caused by RPGR mutations — the most common cause of X-linked RP.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

Optogenetics — Restoring Vision in End-Stage RP

Delivers light-sensitive proteins to surviving retinal cells that have lost their natural photoreceptors. Mutation-agnostic, so could help many RP types. Nanoscope MCO-010 (the most advanced programme) is in a rolling FDA Biologics License Application submission with full filing expected in the first half of 2026; three-year follow-up data presented at ARVO 2026 showed lasting improvements. GenSight GS030, Bionic Sight BS01 and others have ongoing Phase 1/2 trials. Could help people with advanced RP who are not eligible for gene therapy.

Visit website ↗

🌅 Horizon

CRISPR Gene Editing for RP

Early-stage CRISPR trials targeting specific RP mutations. Unlike gene replacement, CRISPR can correct the faulty gene in place. The first-in-human CRISPR trial for RP (ZVS203e) is recruiting patients with RHO mutations (the most common cause of autosomal dominant RP) in a Phase 1/2 study. Significant long-term potential but very early stage.

Practical guides

Getting a Genetic Test

Ask your ophthalmologist for a referral to an Inherited Retinal Disease clinic. Genetic testing is free on the NHS. Your result determines eligibility for treatments including Luxturna.

Long Cane Training

The single most effective mobility aid for RP. Free via your local authority rehabilitation service — ask your council's sensory impairment team or ophthalmologist for a referral.

Applying for a Guide Dog

Apply via guidedogs.org.uk. A guide dog is completely free — all costs including vet bills are covered. The process takes 6–18 months but transforms independent travel.

Helpful technology

Filters

Amber / Tinted Filter Glasses

Amber and orange tints cut glare and blue light, improving contrast and comfort in changing light — particularly helpful with the light sensitivity common in RP. Available from low-vision opticians and specialist suppliers; ask about a free low-vision assessment first.

Field Expansion

Field-Expansion Prisms

For tunnel vision, an optometrist can fit Fresnel or ground-in prisms that shift more of the surrounding scene into your remaining central field, helping with obstacle awareness. Best fitted by a low-vision specialist as part of an assessment.

AI Wearable

OrCam MyEye (reading & recognition camera)

In advanced RP, when reading becomes difficult, this clip-on camera reads text aloud and recognises faces and products — studies in advanced RP show real quality-of-life benefit. Clips to any glasses frame.

Screen Reader

Screen Readers (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA)

As sight reduces, screen readers let you use a phone or computer by listening instead of looking. VoiceOver (iPhone/iPad) and TalkBack (Android) are built in and free; NVDA for Windows is a free download; JAWS is the paid professional option.

Navigation App

Talking Navigation Apps

Apps like Google Maps and Soundscape give spoken, step-by-step walking directions and audio cues, useful when peripheral vision makes spotting landmarks hard. Works with your phone's screen reader.

Free App

Seeing AI / Be My Eyes

Seeing AI describes scenes, reads text, and identifies people, products and currency through the camera. Be My Eyes connects you live to a sighted volunteer or company agent who can describe what your camera sees.

Guide Dog

Guide Dogs UK

A trained guide dog provides safe, confident independent travel — valuable as tunnel vision and night blindness progress. Completely free including all ongoing costs. Apply at guidedogs.org.uk.

Benefits & registration

CVI Registration

Peripheral field loss from RP meets criteria for partial sight or blind registration. Register early — it unlocks significant support.

PIP — Daily Living and Mobility

RP significantly affects both daily living and mobility, especially at night. Both components of PIP often apply.

Motability Scheme

PIP enhanced mobility recipients can lease a car, powered wheelchair, or scooter via Motability.

Living with Retinitis Pigmentosa

Night Vision and Lighting

Install motion-sensor night lights throughout your home — hallways, stairs, bathroom. Carry a small torch or use your phone torch when out at night. Allow extra time for your eyes to adjust when moving from bright to dark environments — this takes significantly longer with RP.

Long Cane and Guide Dogs

A long cane is the single most effective mobility aid for RP and training is free via your local authority. A guide dog transforms independent travel — guidedogs.org.uk. Neither is a sign of giving up — both are tools that restore independence and confidence.

Tunnel Vision Strategies

Scan widely and deliberately — turn your head and body to compensate for narrowing peripheral vision. Sit with your back to a wall in restaurants and public spaces to reduce the need to monitor your sides. Inform people you're with about your visual field so they can position themselves appropriately.

Employment

RP progresses — plan ahead with your employer rather than waiting for a crisis. A workplace needs assessment, screen magnification, adjusted lighting, and flexible working are all reasonable adjustments. Access to Work funds specialist equipment and support workers.

Sport and Activity

British Blind Sport has clubs for guide running, tandem cycling, goalball, and more. Staying physically active is important for wellbeing — and many people with RP describe sport as transformative for their confidence and mental health. britishblindsport.org.uk

Support organisations

Retina UK

UK's specialist RP charity. Helpline, support groups, family weekends, and the largest UK funder of RP research.
