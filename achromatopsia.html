What is Achromatopsia?

Achromatopsia (rod monochromacy) is a rare inherited retinal condition affecting around 1 in 30,000 people. It is caused by non-functioning cone photoreceptors, meaning people see only in black, white, and grey and have severe light sensitivity.

How it affects vision

Colour vision is completely absent. Visual acuity is typically 6/60 or worse. Intense photophobia severely reduces vision in normal lighting. Nystagmus is present in most cases. Dark-adapted vision is normal — people often see better in dim light than bright daylight.

Getting a diagnosis

Diagnosed by electroretinography (ERG) showing absent cone responses with normal rod responses, visual acuity testing, and genetic testing.

Who it affects

Affects approximately 1 in 30,000 people. Both sexes affected equally. Autosomal recessive. Often identified in early infancy due to light sensitivity and nystagmus.

Research & treatment progress

🔬 Pipeline

CNGB3 Gene Therapy (Most Common Form)

CNGB3 mutations cause around half of achromatopsia cases. Several Phase 1/2 trials (including UK programmes from MeiraGTx) have shown good safety and modest improvements in light sensitivity and some visual measures, mainly in the higher-dose groups. This is the more advanced of the achromatopsia gene therapy programmes. A genetic diagnosis confirming CNGB3 is needed for eligibility.

🔬 Pipeline

CNGA3 Gene Therapy

CNGA3 mutations are the second most common cause of achromatopsia. Phase 1/2 trials have confirmed safety, but improvements in vision have so far been less consistent than for CNGB3 — one programme (AGTC-402) was discontinued after limited benefit, while UK academic and MeiraGTx work continues. Research is ongoing to understand and improve the response.

🌅 Horizon

Cone Cell Regeneration

Longer-term research into regenerating lost or non-functional cone cells using gene therapy combined with cellular approaches. Very early stage.

Practical guides

Tinted Lenses and Filter Glasses

Red or plum-tinted lenses dramatically reduce photophobia and improve functional vision in bright conditions. Specialist providers include Eschenbach and NoIR Medical Technologies.

Getting a Genetic Test

Knowing your specific gene mutation is essential to access current and future gene therapy trials. Ask your ophthalmologist for a referral to an inherited retinal disease clinic.

Lighting Adjustments at School or Work

Reducing overhead lighting, using window blinds, and allowing tinted lenses indoors are all reasonable adjustments under the Equality Act 2010.

Helpful technology

Filter Lenses

Red / Dark Tinted Filter Glasses

Deeply tinted absorptive lenses — often red or plum-tinted — are the single most helpful aid in achromatopsia, dramatically cutting light sensitivity and improving comfort and function outdoors. Specialist brands include NoIR, Eschenbach and Zeiss; best chosen with a low-vision optometrist.

Filter Lenses

Tinted Contact Lenses

Some people benefit from red or amber tinted contact lenses (sometimes with a clear central pupil zone) to reduce glare while keeping a normal appearance. Fitted by a specialist contact-lens optometrist.

Magnification

Magnifiers (Optical & Electronic)

Because visual acuity is reduced, magnification helps with reading. Simple optical magnifiers may be loaned free via an NHS low-vision clinic; electronic handheld magnifiers add adjustable contrast and colour modes.

Screen Settings

Dark Mode & Brightness Control

Turn on dark mode across phone, tablet and computer and lower brightness; tools like f.lux can dim a screen further than the system minimum and warm the colour to reduce glare.

Free App

Seeing AI / Be My Eyes

Seeing AI reads text and describes scenes through the camera; Be My Eyes connects you to a sighted volunteer. Both help when small print or glare make a task difficult.

Benefits & registration

Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI)

Most people with achromatopsia qualify for partial sight or sight impairment registration. Ask at your next appointment.

PIP

Both Daily Living and Mobility components may apply. Photophobia and reduced acuity significantly limit daily activities.

Access to Work

Covers tinted lenses, specialist screen equipment, and lighting adjustments. Apply at gov.uk/access-to-work.

Living with Achromatopsia

Managing Light Sensitivity

Red or plum tinted lenses are essential outdoors and in bright indoor environments. Dark mode on all devices, window blinds, and reduced overhead lighting at work or school are all critical adjustments. A gradual transition between light levels helps — pause when entering bright environments to allow adjustment.

Screens and Technology

Set all devices to dark mode, minimum brightness, and large text. f.lux or Night Shift can dim screens below system minimum. Coloured overlays on printed material can sometimes improve readability. Screen glare filters on monitors significantly reduce discomfort.

School and Work Adjustments

Shaded seating away from windows, permission to wear tinted lenses indoors, and reduced overhead lighting are all reasonable adjustments. The earlier these are established — ideally from the start of school — the better the experience for the child.

The Social Side

Achromatopsia is rare and often misunderstood. People may not understand why you struggle in bright light or why you cannot see colour. Retina UK's peer support network connects people with achromatopsia and their families across the UK.

Research and Trials

Gene therapy trials for CNGB3 and CNGA3 are the most active research area for achromatopsia. Getting a genetic diagnosis is essential for trial eligibility. The US-based Foundation Fighting Blindness runs a patient registry that notifies registered families of relevant international trials.

For Parents — Achromatopsia

Achromatopsia is usually identified in early infancy due to light sensitivity and nystagmus. Here is what matters most for your child.

Managing Light Sensitivity

Light sensitivity (photophobia) is often the most immediately challenging aspect of achromatopsia for a young child. Red or plum tinted lenses — prescribed by your low vision clinic — dramatically reduce discomfort. Your child should wear these outdoors from the earliest age and be permitted to wear them indoors at school.

Your Child Sees a Full World

Achromatopsia means no colour vision and reduced acuity, but your child will navigate their world with confidence. Many adults with achromatopsia describe seeing in greyscale as simply the way they have always seen — not as a loss. They see a rich, detailed world, just differently.

Genetic Testing for Trials

Gene therapy trials for CNGB3 and CNGA3 — the two most common achromatopsia genes — are progressing. Getting a genetic diagnosis now ensures your child is positioned for trial eligibility. Free on the NHS — ask your ophthalmologist.

School Adjustments

Shaded seating away from windows, permission to wear tinted lenses indoors, reduced overhead lighting, and a tablet for close-up work are all reasonable adjustments. Establish these from the start of school.

Peer Support via Retina UK

Retina UK runs peer support groups for families affected by inherited retinal conditions including achromatopsia. Connecting with other families — particularly those with older children living well — is enormously helpful. retinauk.org.uk or 0300 111 4000.
