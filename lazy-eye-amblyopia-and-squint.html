Lazy Eye (Amblyopia) and Squint

What is Lazy Eye (Amblyopia) and Squint?

Lazy eye (amblyopia) is when vision in one eye does not develop properly; rarely, both eyes are affected. A squint (strabismus) is when the eyes do not line up and point in different directions. One eye may turn in, out, up or down while the other looks straight ahead. A squint can lead to a lazy eye but does not always. Both are seen most often in young children, though a squint can develop at any age.

How it affects vision

A lazy eye often has no symptoms and is found during an eye test. Signs in a child can include closing one eye, eyes pointing in different directions, difficulty following objects, tilting the head, headaches, clumsiness or frequent tripping. A squint can come with double vision. If untreated in childhood, the loss of sight can be permanent. A squint that develops in an adult, or double vision at any age, needs prompt assessment.

Getting a diagnosis

An optometrist usually checks for lazy eye during an eye test, looking at lights or letters through different lenses and sometimes using eye drops to examine the back of the eye. A GP, health visitor or optician can refer you to an eye specialist.

Who it affects

Mainly children. The cause of a squint is often unknown; it can be present from birth or develop later, and can run in families. In children it is often the eye's response to an underlying vision problem such as short-sightedness, long-sightedness or astigmatism. Rarer causes include some infections, genetic conditions, cerebral palsy and other brain or nerve problems. Rarely, a squint can be a sign of retinoblastoma, a childhood eye cancer, so a child with a squint should see a GP.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Glasses, Patching and Eye Drops

The aim is to improve vision in the weaker eye. Treatment may include glasses, an eye patch over the stronger eye for a few hours a day for several months (usually with glasses), or eye drops that temporarily blur the stronger eye. Treatment should ideally start before the age of 7, while vision is still developing.

✅ NHS Available

Squint Treatment

Treatment is usually recommended because a squint is unlikely to resolve on its own. Options include glasses (if the squint stems from a vision problem such as long-sightedness), eye exercises, surgery to adjust the eye muscles when glasses alone aren't enough, and muscle injections that temporarily weaken eye muscles to improve alignment. If a lazy eye is also present it is usually treated first. Squint surgery straightens the eyes but does not improve vision.

Practical guides

Get Your Child's Eyes Tested Early

Lazy eye often has no obvious signs. Ask an optician to test your child's eyes if you notice head tilting, closing one eye, eyes pointing in different directions, or clumsiness, or if they haven't had a test in two years. NHS sight tests are free for children under 16 and for 16 to 18-year-olds in full-time education.

See a GP About a Squint

A child with a squint that is always present, or one that comes and goes after 3 months of age, should see a GP, health visitor or optician. Occasional squints in babies under 3 months are common and usually not a concern.

Stick With Patching and Glasses

Patching works best when it is done as advised for the full time. Ask your eye clinic for tips if your child finds it hard.

Act Before Age 7

Treatment works best when started early, ideally before the age of 7. If your child is older than 7, ask the clinic what is possible.

Squint in Adults

A squint or double vision that develops in adulthood needs prompt assessment by a GP or optician and an eye specialist. Surgery can still improve how the eyes line up after many years.

Benefits & registration

Free NHS Sight Tests

Sight tests are free on the NHS if you are aged 60 or over (throughout the UK), and for everyone in Scotland. They are also free for people with diabetes or glaucoma, people registered as sight impaired, and some others, including children under 16. Ask your optician.

Optical Vouchers for Glasses

Children under 16 and 16 to 18-year-olds in full-time education may qualify for NHS optical vouchers towards glasses or contact lenses. Ask your optician.

Disability Benefits for Children

Most children with a lazy eye or squint don't qualify, but if sight is significantly affected, ask about Disability Living Allowance (in Scotland, Child Disability Payment). It depends on how much extra help the child needs, not the diagnosis.

Living with Lazy Eye (Amblyopia) and Squint

Support at School

Tell the school early. Preferential seating and reduced copying from the board are straightforward reasonable adjustments, and your local authority's sensory support service can advise. Your local SENDIASS gives free independent advice.

Confidence and Wellbeing

A visible squint can affect a child's or adult's confidence. Ask your eye clinic about treatment options and, if teasing is a problem, speak to the school.

Driving

You must meet the legal eyesight standard to drive and tell the DVLA if you do not. Double vision from a squint can make driving unsafe. Ask your optician or eye clinic if unsure.
