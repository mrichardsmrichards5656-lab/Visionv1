What is Glaucoma?

Glaucoma is a group of eye conditions that damage the optic nerve, usually caused by elevated pressure inside the eye. It is the second most common cause of blindness in the UK, affecting over 700,000 people. Half of those affected don't know they have it — making regular eye tests essential.

How it affects vision

Peripheral vision is gradually lost, often without symptoms until significant damage has occurred. In advanced stages, tunnel vision develops. Acute angle-closure glaucoma causes sudden severe eye pain, headache, and nausea — a medical emergency.

Getting a diagnosis

Detected via eye pressure measurement, optic nerve examination, visual field tests, and OCT scanning. All adults should have an eye test every two years.

Who it affects

Risk is significantly higher over 60, in people of Black African or Caribbean heritage, those with a first-degree relative with glaucoma, high myopia, and diabetes.

Research & treatment progress

🌍 Available Abroad

iDose TR (Travoprost) Implant

A tiny implant placed inside the eye delivers continuous pressure-lowering medication for up to 3 years — eliminating daily eye drops. FDA approved December 2023. No MHRA or NICE approval announced for the UK as of 2026. Speak to your ophthalmologist if you have difficulty with eye drops — alternatives like SLT laser may be available.

🔬 Pipeline

AI Progression Detection on OCT Scans

Active research is developing AI models that detect glaucoma progression from OCT scans earlier than standard methods. Moorfields, UCL and international groups have published deep-learning approaches with high accuracy, but most are still in research or limited pilot use rather than full NHS deployment. Ask your eye clinic whether AI-assisted OCT analysis is being used.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

Neuroprotection Research

Trials investigating whether drugs can protect optic nerve fibres from dying — going beyond just lowering pressure. Active Phase 2 programmes include NT-501 (Neurotech, an implant releasing CNTF protein), BL1107 (Bausch + Lomb, alpha-2 agonist) and nicotinamide riboside oral supplementation. Larger results expected through 2026-27.

Visit website ↗

🌅 Horizon

Gene Therapy for Glaucoma

Early research into gene therapy approaches targeting the trabecular meshwork (the eye's drainage system) to permanently improve fluid drainage and reduce IOP without daily medication. A first-in-human CRISPR/Cas9 trial began in 2024 for the small group of patients with myocilin (MYOC) gene mutations — about 4% of adult open-angle glaucoma. Wider gene therapies remain at preclinical stage.

Practical guides

Using Your Eye Drops Correctly

Tilt your head back, pull down your lower lid, look up. Squeeze one drop in. Close your eye for two minutes and press the inner corner. Adherence is the single most important thing to protect your vision.

Getting Your Family Tested

First-degree relatives of glaucoma patients are around four times more likely to develop glaucoma. Those aged 40 and over are entitled to free NHS eye tests. Tell your family now — glaucoma is silent until significant damage has occurred.

Applying for Benefits

Visual field loss from glaucoma supports both Daily Living and Mobility PIP components. RNIB's benefits team provides free form-filling support — 0303 123 9999.

Helpful technology

Eye Drop Aid

Eye-Drop Dispensing Aids (Autodrop, Opticare)

Simple plastic aids that hold the bottle, steady your aim and squeeze for you — making daily drops easier and improving adherence, which is the single most important factor in protecting your sight. Ask your pharmacist or eye clinic; some are available on prescription.

Reminders

Medication Reminder Apps & Alarms

Glaucoma drops only work if taken consistently. A free phone reminder or a dedicated app (e.g. Medisafe) prompts you at the right time and tracks doses.

Lighting & Contrast

Task Lighting & High-Contrast Settings

Good directional lighting plus high-contrast and dark-mode settings on devices make the most of remaining vision as the field narrows. Built into all phones, tablets and computers at no cost.

Magnification

Magnifiers (Optical & Electronic)

For reading once vision is affected, optical magnifiers may be loaned free via an NHS low-vision clinic; electronic handheld magnifiers add contrast and colour modes.

Navigation

Talking Navigation & Mobility Aids

As peripheral field narrows, spoken sat-nav apps (Google Maps, Soundscape) help with orientation, and an orientation-and-mobility assessment can advise on a symbol or long cane. Apps are free.

Free App

Seeing AI / Be My Eyes

Read text and identify objects (Seeing AI) or get live help from a sighted volunteer (Be My Eyes) through your phone camera.

Benefits & registration

Free NHS Eye Tests

As a glaucoma patient, you are entitled to free NHS eye tests. Your first-degree relatives (parents, siblings, children) aged 40+ are also entitled to free NHS sight tests — make sure they know. Register this with your optician.

PIP for Glaucoma

Visual field loss supports a PIP claim. Both Daily Living and Mobility components may apply depending on severity.

Workplace Adjustments

Under the Equality Act 2010, employers must make reasonable adjustments. RNIB's Employment Line (0303 123 9999) provides free confidential advice.

Living with Glaucoma

Eye Drop Routine

Take drops at the same time every day — link them to an existing routine like brushing teeth. If you miss a dose, take it as soon as you remember but never double up. An Autodrop device ensures accurate delivery every time and is available from RNIB Shop for around £15.

Driving with Glaucoma

Visual field loss from glaucoma may affect your legal ability to drive. You must inform the DVLA. The DVLA will arrange a visual field assessment — some people with glaucoma can continue driving, others cannot. Get clarity from your ophthalmologist.

Peripheral Vision Strategies

Peripheral vision loss makes navigating busy environments and detecting movement at the sides difficult. Scan actively by turning your head rather than relying on peripheral awareness. Good lighting significantly helps — install night lights on stairs and in hallways.

Family Screening

Tell your first-degree relatives — they are around four times more likely to develop glaucoma. Those aged 40 and over are entitled to free NHS eye tests. This is one of the most important things you can do. Many people with glaucoma feel a real sense of responsibility to protect their family's sight.

Stress and Eye Pressure

Sustained stress and certain positions (like playing certain wind instruments or inverted yoga poses) can temporarily raise eye pressure. Discuss any concerns with your ophthalmologist. Regular, moderate exercise generally helps maintain healthy eye pressure.
