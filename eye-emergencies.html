Eye Emergencies

Some eye problems need treatment within hours to protect your sight. This page gives general NHS-based guidance on what needs an emergency response, what needs help the same day, and what can wait. It cannot diagnose you. If you are unsure, get advice now rather than waiting.

🚨 Call 999 or go to A&E now

  • Sudden loss of sight in one eye, especially if painless, even if it comes back within minutes. It can be an "eye stroke" or a warning of a stroke.
  • Sudden sight loss with a drooping face, weak arm, slurred speech or confusion (stroke signs)
  • A strong chemical in your eye, such as bleach or oven cleaner (rinse first, see below)
  • A sharp object has pierced your eye, or something hit your eye at high speed (power tools, mowing)
  • Your sight changes after an eye injury
  • Severe eye pain, or eye pain with headache, sensitivity to light, feeling sick or vomiting
  • You cannot move your eye or keep it open
  • Blood or pus coming from the eye

Do not drive yourself. Ask someone to take you or call an ambulance. Take a list of your medicines. Do not try to remove anything that has pierced the eye, and do not rub or touch the eye.

💧 Chemical in the eye: rinse straight away

While you wait for help, hold the eye open and run plenty of clean, not-hot water gently over it for at least 20 minutes. Use a tap, shower or bottled water. Take the container with you to hospital.

⏱️ Get help today (do not wait for a routine appointment)

Contact NHS 111 (call or use 111 online) or an emergency eye service if you have:

  • New floaters, flashes of light, or a dark curtain or shadow across your vision. These can be early signs of a retinal tear or detachment.
  • Sudden blurring of vision
  • A red, painful eye with light sensitivity or reduced vision
  • New double vision
  • A red, painful eye while wearing contact lenses: take them out and do not put them back in until you have been seen
  • If you are over 50, a new severe headache with jaw pain when chewing, scalp tenderness or sight changes (could be giant cell arteritis)
  • A child under 5 with an eye problem: NHS advice is to always call 111

Where to call: NHS 111 in England and Wales; NHS 24 on 111 in Scotland. In Northern Ireland, contact your GP or out-of-hours service, or go to an emergency department. In some areas opticians and eye casualty departments offer urgent same-day eye care, and 111 can point you to it.

🗓️ Can wait a few days

Book an optician or GP appointment for a gradual change in vision, mild irritation, tired or gritty eyes, or an injury that is not improving after 24 hours. If you are worried, or the pain is getting worse, call 111 instead.
