What is Leber Congenital Amaurosis?

Leber Congenital Amaurosis (LCA) is a group of severe inherited retinal dystrophies and the most common cause of inherited blindness in childhood, affecting around 1 in 50,000 births. Multiple genes can cause LCA — the most significant being CEP290, GUCY2D, and RPE65.

How it affects vision

Babies typically show rapid involuntary eye movements (nystagmus), lack of visual responsiveness, and may press on their eyes. Visual acuity is severely reduced from the first months of life. Photophobia and extreme farsightedness are common.

Getting a diagnosis

Electroretinography (ERG) showing markedly reduced or absent responses. Genetic testing is essential and guides treatment eligibility. Referral to a specialist inherited retinal disease service is important from the first months of life.

Who it affects

Present from birth or early infancy. Affects approximately 1 in 50,000 worldwide. The most common cause of inherited visual impairment in children. Autosomal recessive in most cases.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Luxturna — RPE65-LCA (Available Now)

A single subretinal injection in each eye. Life-changing vision improvements in eligible patients. Available on the NHS at specialist centres. Getting a genetic diagnosis is essential — this treatment only works for RPE65 mutations.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

EDIT-101 — CEP290 Gene Editing (Development Paused)

Uses CRISPR to edit the most common CEP290 mutation (IVS26), which cannot be treated with Luxturna. The Phase 1/2 BRILLIANCE trial showed proof of concept — 3 of 14 treated patients had meaningful vision improvement, mainly those with two copies of the IVS26 mutation. Editas paused enrolment in 2022 and in 2023 stepped back from its inherited retinal disease programmes to focus elsewhere, seeking a partner to take EDIT-101 forward. No active recruitment at present.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

ATSN-101 — GUCY2D Gene Therapy (LCA1)

Atsena Therapeutics' gene replacement therapy for LCA1, caused by GUCY2D mutations (around 20% of LCA cases). A completed Phase 1/2 trial showed clinically meaningful, durable vision gains — three-year data (ARVO 2026) reported roughly a 100-fold improvement in light sensitivity at the high dose, with no drug-related serious adverse events. A global pivotal Phase 3 trial is expected to begin in the second half of 2026.

Visit website ↗

🔬 Pipeline

AAV-AIPL1 — UK Breakthrough for LCA4

A landmark UK gene therapy for LCA4 (caused by AIPL1 mutations), developed at Moorfields, UCL and Great Ormond Street with MeiraGTx. All 11 children treated (aged 1-4, legally blind from birth) gained measurable vision after a single subretinal injection — results published in The Lancet (2025). Treatment must happen very early, before the retina degenerates. MeiraGTx (now partnered with Eli Lilly) plans to seek regulatory approval based on these results.

Practical guides

Get a Genetic Diagnosis as Early as Possible

Different LCA genes respond to different treatments. Ask for urgent referral to a paediatric inherited retinal disease clinic. Genetic testing is free on the NHS.

Early Intervention and Habilitation

Children with LCA benefit enormously from early specialist education. Contact your local authority to request an EHC plan and a Qualified Teacher of Visual Impairment (QTVI) as early as possible.

Connecting with Other Families

Families affected by LCA describe connecting with the LCA community as transformative. Retina UK can connect you with other families and trial news in the UK.

Claiming DLA for Your Child

Children under 16 with LCA are eligible for Disability Living Allowance (DLA) — potentially worth over £600/month. Call RNIB (0303 123 9999) for free help with the application.

Helpful technology

Free Built-In

VoiceOver / TalkBack

Apple's VoiceOver (iPhone/iPad) and Google's TalkBack (Android) are full screen readers built into every device at no cost, letting a child or adult use a phone or tablet entirely by listening. Set up in Settings → Accessibility.

Screen Reader

NVDA / JAWS for Windows

NVDA is a free, open-source Windows screen reader that does everything most people need. JAWS is the long-established professional option, now sold mainly as an annual or perpetual subscription (price varies — check Freedom Scientific or VisionAid); Access to Work or Disabled Students' Allowance often funds it for work or study.

Braille

Refreshable Braille Display

Translates on-screen text into tactile braille in real time, and pairs with a phone or computer. Budget models like the Orbit Reader 20 are the affordable end; 40-cell and notetaker models cost more. DSA or Access to Work funding is often available.

Navigation

Talking Navigation & Smart Speakers

Spoken sat-nav apps (Google Maps, Microsoft Soundscape) aid independent travel, and a smart speaker (Alexa, Google) gives hands-free, eyes-free access to information, reminders, music and smart-home control at home.

Free App

Seeing AI / Be My Eyes

Seeing AI reads text and describes scenes, people and products through the camera; Be My Eyes connects to a sighted volunteer for live help. Both are free and widely used by people with little or no vision.

Benefits & registration

Disability Living Allowance (DLA) — Children

Children under 16 with LCA are eligible. Can be worth £600+ per month. Call RNIB (0303 123 9999) for free help.

PIP (from age 16)

At 16, DLA transitions to PIP. Both enhanced rate components apply for most people with LCA.

Education, Health and Care Plan (EHC)

Children are entitled to an EHC plan funding specialist educational support, IT equipment, and braille resources.

Disabled Students Allowance (DSA)

For students in higher education. Funds screen readers, braille equipment, and study support.

Living with Leber Congenital Amaurosis

Early Intervention is Everything

The earlier a child with LCA receives specialist education, habilitation, and technology training, the better their long-term outcomes. Push hard for a QTVI (Qualified Teacher of Visual Impairment) from the first year of life — local authorities must provide this under law.

Braille and Technology

Both braille and screen reader technology open the world for people with LCA. They are not alternatives — many people use both. A refreshable braille display connected to a phone or computer provides silent, tactile access to everything. VoiceOver (iPhone) and TalkBack (Android) are free and powerful.

Education and the EHC Plan

An Education, Health and Care plan is a legal document that must reflect your child's actual needs. Review it every year and challenge any reductions in support. The RNIB and VIEW charity can advise on what to request and how to challenge decisions.

Independence and Travel

Orientation and mobility training — learning to navigate safely with a cane or guide dog — is transformative for independence. This is free via your local authority. Guide dog applications can be made from age 16 — guidedogs.org.uk.

Connecting with Others

The LCA community is small but active and connected. Retina UK has UK-based family networks; Foundation Fighting Blindness (US-based) runs an international community. Meeting other families living well with LCA is one of the most powerful things newly diagnosed families describe.

For Parents — Leber Congenital Amaurosis

LCA is present from birth. If your baby or young child has been diagnosed, you are in the right place. Here is what matters most right now.

You Will Find Your Way

Parenting a child with LCA is different — but it is not lesser. Children born with LCA grow up knowing their world through touch, sound, and the vision they have. They develop independence, confidence, and resilience in ways that will continually surprise you.

Genetic Testing is Urgent

Different LCA genes respond to different treatments. Luxturna — an NHS-approved gene therapy — is available for RPE65 mutations and has produced life-changing results. Getting a genetic diagnosis as early as possible determines what treatments your child may be eligible for. Ask for urgent referral to a paediatric inherited retinal disease clinic.

Early Habilitation

Request a QTVI from your local authority immediately — from the first months of life. Early specialist support for a child with LCA is not just helpful, it is transformative. The earlier it starts, the better the outcomes.

EHC Plan

Apply for an Education, Health and Care plan from your local authority before your child starts any formal education. This legally binding document funds specialist support, technology, and teaching assistance throughout their education.

DLA

Your child is entitled to Disability Living Allowance at the higher rate for both Care and Mobility. This can be worth over £600/month. Call RNIB (0303 123 9999) for free help with the form — it is complex but you are fully entitled to it.
