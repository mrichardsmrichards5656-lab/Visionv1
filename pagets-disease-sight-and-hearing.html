What is Paget's Disease (Sight & Hearing)?

Paget's disease of bone is a chronic condition affecting how bones renew themselves, leading to bones that are larger but weaker than normal. While it is primarily a bone condition, when it affects the bones of the skull it can press on the nerves that serve sight and hearing — causing vision loss, hearing loss, or both. On Sight Atlas we focus on these sensory complications. For the bone side of Paget's, the Paget's Association is the leading UK charity (see support links below).

How it affects vision

When Paget's affects the skull, complications can include: hearing loss (the most common neurological complication) — usually gradual, often affecting both ears; vision changes — less common, but can occur if the optic nerve is compressed (blurred vision, reduced visual field, in rare cases sudden vision loss); headaches, dizziness, balance problems, or tinnitus (ringing in the ears). It's important to know: most people with Paget's disease do not develop sight or hearing problems. The bone changes alone (often in the pelvis, spine, legs) are far more common than skull involvement.

Getting a diagnosis

Paget's itself is diagnosed through blood tests (raised alkaline phosphatase) and bone scans, usually after symptoms like bone pain or following an incidental finding. If sensory complications are suspected, you should be referred for: a full hearing test (audiology) to detect any hearing loss; an eye examination by an ophthalmologist including visual field testing; and MRI or CT imaging of the skull to check whether bones are pressing on the optic or auditory nerves.

Who it affects

Paget's disease affects around 1 in 100 people over 55 in the UK, making it one of the more common bone disorders in older adults. It's more common in men, more common with family history, and more common in people of British, European, Australian, or New Zealand descent. Sight and hearing complications are rare — affecting only a minority of people with skull involvement. Diagnosis and treatment have improved significantly in recent decades, meaning serious complications are less common than they once were.

Research & treatment progress

✅ NHS Available

Bisphosphonates (Zoledronate, Risedronate)

Modern bisphosphonates given as an infusion (zoledronate) or tablets (risedronate) are highly effective at suppressing the overactive bone turnover in Paget's, and are the main treatment for pagetic bone pain. Available on the NHS for people meeting criteria. Note that while they reliably lower bone turnover markers, trials have not shown that they prevent the complications of Paget's such as deafness, fracture or deformity — so treatment decisions are usually guided by symptoms rather than by blood results alone.

✅ NHS Available

Regular Monitoring for Sensory Changes

If skull bones are affected, annual hearing tests and eye examinations can catch nerve compression early. Early treatment can prevent further loss. Ask your specialist team to coordinate with audiology and ophthalmology.

Practical guides

Get Both Sight and Hearing Checked

If you have Paget's affecting the skull, ask your GP for referrals to both audiology and ophthalmology. Early monitoring catches changes before they become significant. Sensory complications can develop slowly — regular checks matter more than waiting for symptoms.

Understanding Bisphosphonate Treatment

Bisphosphonates are very effective at suppressing the overactive bone turnover in Paget's, and zoledronate can give years of disease control from a single infusion. They are mainly used to treat bone pain. It is worth being clear about what they have not been shown to do: the large UK PRISM trial (1,324 patients) found that treating intensively to normalise blood markers made no difference to hearing thresholds, fractures or quality of life compared with treating symptoms as they arose, and a follow-on study found no advantage either. There is currently no good evidence that bisphosphonates prevent the sight or hearing complications of Paget's. This does not mean treatment is pointless — it means the decision should be based on your symptoms and your specialist's assessment rather than on preventing sensory loss. Discuss the reasons for treatment with your specialist, and follow their advice.

Tinnitus and Balance

Many people with skull involvement experience tinnitus or balance problems before frank hearing loss. Mention any new ringing, fullness, or dizziness to your GP — these can be early signs that treatment review is needed.

Combined Sight and Hearing Loss

If you develop both sight and hearing changes, you may qualify for additional support as a person with dual sensory loss. Deafblind UK and Sense both provide specialist help (see support links below). This is the same support pathway used for Usher syndrome.

Helpful technology

Hearing

Hearing Aids — NHS

Paget's affecting the skull can cause progressive hearing loss. NHS hearing aids are free and can be programmed to your specific pattern of loss. Self-refer via your GP or local audiology service.

Vibrating Alert

Vibrating Alert Systems

If hearing is affected, vibrating pagers and pads alert you to the doorbell, phone or smoke alarm through touch. Available from deaf-equipment suppliers.

Sight

Low-Vision Aids & Magnifiers

If sight is affected (less common, from pressure on the optic nerve), a free NHS low-vision assessment provides magnifiers, lighting advice and tailored recommendations. Optical aids are often loaned free; electronic handheld magnifiers add contrast and colour.

Combined

Dual-Sensory Communicator-Guides

If both senses are affected, communicator-guides from Sense and Deafblind UK help with communication, getting out and about, and accessing information — the same support pathway used for Usher syndrome.

Benefits & registration

Attendance Allowance or PIP

If Paget's complications significantly affect daily life — through pain, mobility, sight loss, or hearing loss — you may qualify for Personal Independence Payment (under 66) or Attendance Allowance (66+). Contact RNIB or Citizens Advice for support with the application.

Free NHS Hearing Aids

NHS hearing aids are free of charge. Self-refer to audiology via your GP. Replacement and adjustments are also free for life.

CVI Registration

If sight has been significantly affected, ask your ophthalmologist about Certificate of Vision Impairment (CVI) registration. This unlocks Blue Badge, council tax reduction, and other support.

Free Prescriptions

People over 60 in England get free prescriptions automatically. Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland have free prescriptions for everyone.
